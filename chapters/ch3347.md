## Chapter 3347 — Stolen

The subject was a difficult one to broach, but Fu Zhun was not one to dither. After a brief hesitation, she said, “I want to talk to you about Lin'er.”

Hearing that name, Zhu Yan frowned and sighed. “After so many years, you still haven't let go?”

Fu Zhun stared straight at him. “Have you?”

Zhu Yan said, “What does it matter if I haven't? Things have already turned out this way. We have to look ahead instead of dwelling on every rut in the road.”

Fu Zhun shook her head. “I simply cannot accept it. You and I both possess Tenth-Tier bloodlines, so why did Lin'er inherit an incomplete Origin? Which of us was at fault?”

Zhu Yan smiled bitterly. “So what if they are Tenth-Tier? The Dragon Clan has always struggled to produce offspring. Haven't there been countless cases like this over the generations?” After a moment's thought, he said, “There is no point discussing this any further. Let the past remain in the past.”

Fu Zhun still shook her head.

Zhu Yan did not know whether to laugh or cry. “Then what do you want to do?”

Fu Zhun lowered her head. “I've always felt that Lin'er didn't die, that one day he would see the light of day again.”

“He is already in the Dragon Tomb.” Zhu Yan rested a hand on Fu Zhun's shoulder and gently urged, “Don't dwell on this anymore.”

Fu Zhun bowed her head even lower, her voice dropping with it. “Lin'er isn't in the Dragon Tomb.”

Zhu Yan froze and looked at her with a strange expression. “What do you mean?”

“Lin'er isn't in the Dragon Tomb,” Fu Zhun repeated. Only then did she raise her head and meet Zhu Yan's eyes. “He's on Dragon Isle.”

Zhu Yan's eyes widened in astonishment. “You mean you didn't put Lin'er in the Dragon Tomb back then?” This was hardly the first time a dragon egg had died. Whenever it happened, the lifeless egg would be placed in the Dragon Tomb. Zhu Yan had never imagined that Fu Zhun would break this rule. It seemed impossible. Anyone else on Dragon Isle might violate the clan rules, but never Fu Zhun.

“Yes!” Fu Zhun nodded.

Zhu Yan's expression darkened, and he barked, “Foolish! If the other clansmen learn of this, how will you ever face them?” She enforced the clan rules upon everyone else, yet had broken them herself. Such conduct was utterly intolerable.

Fu Zhun said nothing. She clearly knew that what she had done was wrong, but she was a mother clinging to the memory of her own child. What did it matter if she was wrong?

“Where did you put him?” Zhu Yan was both angry and amused. He could not believe she had kept this from him all these years. No wonder she had refused to let him accompany her to the Dragon Tomb back then. She had planned the deception from the start, while he had remained laughably oblivious.

Fu Zhun watched him warily. “What are you planning to do?”

Zhu Yan opened his mouth. “What do you think I'm going to do?”

“You are not allowed to put Lin'er into the Dragon Tomb!” Fu Zhun said through clenched teeth.

Zhu Yan snapped, “Even now, you still refuse to see reason? Lin'er... has no hope left. Why keep him somewhere else? The Dragon Tomb is where our Dragon Clan rests for eternity.”

Fu Zhun gave a cold sneer. “I knew I shouldn't have told you.” With that, she turned to leave.

Zhu Yan grabbed her arm.

“Let go!” Fu Zhun turned her head and fixed him with an icy stare.

Zhu Yan flinched as he realized that this woman was truly furious. If he tried to stop her by force, he might enrage her, so he could only release his grip. “Calm down first.”

“I am very calm!”

The corner of Zhu Yan's mouth twitched. After a moment's thought, he asked, “You kept this from me for so many years, so why did you suddenly decide to tell me today?” That was precisely what puzzled him. They knew each other inside and out, so she should have known exactly how he would react. Yet she had come to confess of her own accord. It made little sense.

Fu Zhun pursed her thin lips. After a brief hesitation, she answered, “I don't know.”

“You don't know?” Zhu Yan stared at her, dumbstruck.

Fu Zhun drew a deep breath. “I don't know why, but I've felt restless these past few days, as though something is about to happen.”

Zhu Yan frowned. “Something concerning Lin'er?”

“That's right!” Fu Zhun nodded. “So I want you to come with me to see Lin'er.”

“All right!” Zhu Yan readily agreed.

Fu Zhun, however, said, “But after seeing your attitude just now, I've changed my mind.”

Zhu Yan smiled bitterly. “Rest assured. What happens to Lin'er is your decision. If you are unwilling, I won't force you to put him in the Dragon Tomb.” It was the only answer he could give. Unless he made his position clear, she would never take him to see Lin'er. The important thing now was to find out where Lin'er was. He could deal with everything else when the time came.

“Truly?” Fu Zhun's eyes lit up with delight. Her icy demeanor melted away, revealing a rare touch of gentleness.

“Truly!” Zhu Yan nodded solemnly.

Fu Zhun smiled faintly and beckoned. “Then follow me.” Before she had even finished speaking, she flew off ahead of him.

Zhu Yan did not follow at once. He simply stood there in a daze for a moment.

It had been far, far too many years since he had last seen Fu Zhun smile. For that brief instant, her smile had made the whole world pale in comparison. She had never smiled often, but in the old days, she had at least smiled from time to time. After what happened to Lin'er, however, smiles had vanished from her face entirely. No matter whom she faced or what the occasion, she always wore the frozen expression of a ten-thousand-year-old glacier. The entire Dragon Clan on Dragon Isle feared her.

Why had she put herself through such misery? Zhu Yan sighed inwardly, then flew after her.

The Dragon Clan's two Great Elders arrived together on a nameless island.

Zhu Yan was taken aback. “You kept Lin'er here?”

Fu Zhun pointed ahead. “That's right. There's a natural cave over there. It's well hidden and no one would find it without searching deliberately. Lin'er has been inside all these years.”

Zhu Yan could not help admiring her audacity. This was hardly some deserted corner where no one ever passed. Two clansmen even had Spirit Islands nearby. Sure enough, the most dangerous place was often the safest. They were incredibly fortunate that no one had discovered the secret inside after all these years.

Zhu Yan followed Fu Zhun into the cave, and she led him down the winding passage.

Zhu Yan could tell that Fu Zhun had grown nervous because she had become unusually talkative. He understood how she felt. Was he not the same? Although there was no longer any hope for Lin'er, it would still be good to see him once more.

Tense with anticipation, they made their way to the innermost limestone cavern, where the soft glow of luminous pearls lit the entire chamber. Fu Zhun suddenly stopped, staring blankly at a particular spot as she began to tremble.

Zhu Yan looked around the cavern and frowned. “Where is Lin'er?”

The cavern was completely empty. There was no sign of Lin'er.

He turned and noticed the look on Fu Zhun's face. A sudden realization struck him, and his heart sank.

Just then, Fu Zhun stepped over to the spot where she had left the dragon egg. She searched the area in a daze, then looked up at Zhu Yan. Her eyes reddened, and tears spilled down her cheeks. “Lin'er is gone!”

Zhu Yan asked in a deep voice, “Are you certain you placed Lin'er here?”

Fu Zhun stared blankly ahead. “He was always here. I came to see him a little over half a year ago, and he was still here.” Then something seemed to strike her. Her gaze turned razor-sharp, and murderous intent surged around her as she gritted her teeth. “Who stole him?”

Zhu Yan frowned. “If that's what happened, then one of the clansmen must have taken him. There aren't many of us, so finding the culprit shouldn't be difficult.”

Fu Zhun's voice was glacial. “If I find out who did this, they won't escape my wrath!”

Startled, Zhu Yan hurriedly said, “Perhaps they meant no harm. Don't say such things yet. Once we find out who it was, we can simply have them return him. We can't exactly spread word of this everywhere.”

“Right, right.” Fu Zhun nodded quickly and looked at Zhu Yan. “Go investigate. Hurry! Make them bring Lin'er back at once.”

Zhu Yan gazed at her in silence. Never before had he seen Fu Zhun look so weak and helpless. Only now did he truly understand how important Lin'er was to her.

No wonder she had been feeling restless. Something truly had happened. Once one's cultivation and strength reached their level, one could often sense such events through the unseen workings of fate. It was a profound mystery beyond all explanation.

“What are you still standing there for? Hurry and investigate!” Fu Zhun urged, stamping her foot.

Zhu Yan let out a long sigh. Just how was he supposed to investigate this?

The Dragon Clan had few members, so finding the culprit should have been simple. If one of them had come here and taken Lin'er, Zhu Yan could probably uncover the truth in less than a day. The problem was how to go about it.

He could hardly question every clansman and ask if they had taken a dragon egg from somewhere. Such a question would inevitably arouse suspicion, and then Fu Zhun's secret hoarding of the dragon egg would come to light.

Zhu Yan had to find Lin'er while also protecting Fu Zhun's reputation. It was a difficult balance to strike. He was rather angry himself, too. Just which clansman had done this? After all, his own bloodline lived on in that dragon egg as well.

Meanwhile, on another Spirit Island.

Yang Kai lay coiled around the dragon egg in his Half-Dragon form, shielding it with his own body as he sank into an extremely peculiar state.

Darkness surrounded him without the faintest glimmer of light. He drifted in a stupor, rising and sinking, his consciousness hazy and everything he did driven by instinct.

He tried to stretch out his limbs, only to find himself trapped by something.

A thought surfaced in his mind: Was he inside an egg?

But how could he be inside an egg? He struggled to remember and finally recalled that he had been incubating a dragon egg.

But since he was incubating a dragon egg, why had he ended up inside it?

While he puzzled over this, a distinct consciousness reached out from nearby and tried to draw close to him. Annoyed, Yang Kai pushed it away, but it stubbornly returned and pressed close once more. It even conveyed a sense of yearning, as though he possessed something it desperately needed.

Yang Kai's irritation grew. Just as he was about to repel it again, a thought struck him, and he understood what this consciousness was.

It was the aura of the life inside the dragon egg! Yang Kai was not actually inside an egg. Rather, his consciousness had sunk into the dragon egg and formed a faint resonance with the life within.
